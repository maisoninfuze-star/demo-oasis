# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Furniture shoppers in Laval and Greater Montréal, browsing before they visit the
showroom at 1877 Bd du Curé-Labelle. Two distinct jobs:

- **Furnishing a room.** Wants a complete set, cares about how pieces go
  together, has a budget in mind and a room in mind. Arrives via a department.
- **Replacing one piece.** Knows exactly what they need (a queen bed, a
  nightstand, a 5x8 rug). Wants to filter to it in seconds and see the price.

Both are high-consideration buyers spending hundreds to thousands of dollars,
usually on a phone or laptop at home in the evening, often mid-renovation. Many
are francophone: every surface is FR/EN and must read naturally in both.

The catalogue holds ~7,500 items, 4,631 of them priced. Its whole purpose is
narrowing that to the five the shopper actually wants. Supplier names are never
shown: the store is the brand, and naming Monarch or Matrix on a card just tells
the customer who to buy from instead.

## Product Purpose

Let a shopper go from "I need a bedroom" to a specific priced piece in under a
minute, then either add it to an order or book a showroom visit. Success is a
quote request or an order with the model and SKU already identified, so the team
quotes same-day instead of playing twenty questions.

Price-on-request items exist because the price genuinely depends on fabric,
finish and size. They live in one place, not scattered through the departments,
so the priced catalogue never looks half-finished.

## Brand Personality

Assured, warm, unhurried. A knowledgeable showroom consultant, not a salesperson.
The identity is already set and is not up for redesign: emerald, gold and
warm-black with Cormorant Garamond headings. What changes is the arrangement,
never the character.

## Anti-references

- **Generic Shopify template.** Centred hero, three feature icons, identical
  card grid, stock badges. Interchangeable with a thousand other stores.
- **Cheap liquidation site.** Red sale banners, countdown timers, discount
  badges shouting from every card. This store sells $9,000 bedroom sets.
- **Cold warehouse catalogue.** Spreadsheet density with no warmth, a wholesale
  parts list. Efficient is the goal; sterile is not.
- The current visual identity is explicitly *not* an anti-reference. The owner
  considers it good. Only the arrangement of options is wrong.

## Design Principles

1. **Narrowing is the product.** Every layout decision is judged on whether it
   helps someone cut 4,631 items down faster. Filters are primary UI, not a
   toolbar afterthought.
2. **The price is the answer to the question they came with.** It should be the
   most legible thing on a card after the photo, not a quiet line of small text.
3. **Never make the shopper guess what a thing is.** A card labelled Chair shows
   a chair. A set is named a set. An unpriced item says why and offers the next
   step.
4. **Efficient, still warm.** Density serves the shopper; the emerald-and-gold
   identity keeps it from reading as a wholesale list. Both, not either.
5. **The store is the brand.** No supplier name reaches a customer, anywhere:
   card, filter, alt text, or page source.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Bilingual FR/EN throughout (Québec Bill 96); both languages get equal care.
- Body text ≥4.5:1 contrast on the dark ground; the existing muted greys are the
  known risk area and get checked, not assumed.
- Filters and the quick view are keyboard reachable and screen-reader labelled.
- Reduced-motion alternative for every reveal; the site already respects
  `prefers-reduced-motion` at the splash and must keep doing so.
- Touch targets ≥44px: a large share of traffic is phones.
